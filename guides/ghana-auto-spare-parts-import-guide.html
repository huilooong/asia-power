Ghana Auto Spare Parts Import Guide: Costs, Duties and Best Sources

How Ghanaian importers calculate total landed cost for auto parts from China — FOB, freight, duties and clearing fees explained.

Understanding the Cost Components for Importing Auto Parts

Importing auto spare parts into Ghana from China involves several key cost components that must be carefully calculated to ensure profitability. Understanding these costs will help Ghanaian mechanics and importers manage their budgets effectively.

  • FOB (Free on Board): This is the cost of the auto parts at the manufacturer’s location in China, excluding shipping.
  • Freight Charges: The cost of transporting the goods from the Chinese port to the Ghanaian port, often Tema or Takoradi.
  • Insurance: This cost protects the goods against loss or damage during transit.
  • Duties: Import duties imposed by the Ghana Revenue Authority, which vary depending on the type of product.
  • Clearing and Handling Fees: Costs associated with clearing the goods at the port and handling them to the final destination.

Customs Duties and Taxes in Ghana

When importing auto parts into Ghana, understanding the customs duties and taxes is crucial. The Ghana Revenue Authority oversees these charges, which are generally calculated on the CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight) value of the goods. Importers must pay:

  • Import Duty: Varies by product classification.
  • VAT (Value Added Tax): Currently at 12.5%.
  • NHIL (National Health Insurance Levy) & GETFund Levy: Combined rate of 5%.

Shipping Options from China to Ghana

The choice of shipping method can affect both cost and delivery time. Importers have several options, including:

  • Sea Freight: The most economical option, suitable for large and heavy shipments. Major Ghanaian ports include Tema and Takoradi.
  • Air Freight: Faster but more expensive, ideal for urgent shipments.
