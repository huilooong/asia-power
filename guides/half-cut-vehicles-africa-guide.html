Half-Cut Vehicles for African Mechanics: Everything You Need to Know

What's inside a half-cut shipment, how to order correctly, and how to clear a used half-cut at an African port.

Understanding Half-Cut Vehicles

Half-cut vehicles, commonly referred to in the automotive industry, are vehicles that have been cut horizontally across, usually from the windshield onwards. They are popular among African mechanics and importers due to their cost-effectiveness and the range of usable parts they provide, including the engine, transmission, and front axle. This guide will help you understand what a half-cut shipment includes, how to order correctly, and the relevant customs procedures at African ports.

What is Included in a Half-Cut Shipment?

When you order a half-cut vehicle, you receive the front section of a car. Typically, this includes:

  • Engine: A fully functional engine with accompanying components such as the alternator and starter.
  • Transmission: Gearbox and related mechanisms for gear shifts.
  • Front Axle: Includes wheels, suspension components, and braking systems.
  • Dashboard Accessories: Depending on the condition, items like the steering wheel, airbags, and control panels.
  • Body Panels: Bonnet, fenders, and bumper.

Navigating Customs Procedures

Clearing used vehicle parts through customs can be intricate. Here’s how to manage it effectively:

  • Understand Customs Regulations: Each country has its own regulations. Contact your local customs agency for specific details; for example, the Kenya Revenue Authority or the South African Revenue Service.
  • Required Documentation: Essential documents typically include the bill of lading, invoice, and a certificate of origin.
  • Use a Clearing Agent: Engage local port agents or clearing companies familiar with the process, like Interglobal Freight Clearing in Nigeria or South Coast Logistics in Tanzania.
