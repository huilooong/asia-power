Common BJG buying questions

How should I identify a BJG donor engine?

Start with the BJG marking, then match the model and application date shown above. This source uses a three-letter identification code. Do not assume a four-letter suffix or merge it with similarly named variants. EA211 and EA888 are family labels, not substitutes for the recorded code. Confirm ECU, transmission, emissions equipment and the supplied components against the donor VIN.

What should a seller show before I buy this BJG?

Ask for the actual unit’s identity, donor details, agreed included components and dated condition records. Request test methods and readings, not just “tested” or a claimed mileage. Keep the inspection evidence tied to the offered unit.

Are knocking, smoke or a fault code proof of a common defect?

These are symptoms to investigate, not a diagnosis or a measured failure rate for BJG. Have a qualified workshop establish the cause and scope of repair. This guide does not turn individual owner reports into a universal defect claim.

How to read the inspection evidence

Ask the workshop to record test conditions, engine temperature and cylinder identification. A cold-start video documents that start; it cannot establish long-term oil use. Relative compression compares cylinders and does not provide absolute cylinder pressure. Pico explains the limits and follow-up checks.

Before ordering a replacement, preserve the receiving vehicle’s diagnosis. If the cause of the original failure remains unresolved, fitting another engine may not solve the problem. Service limits, fluids and commissioning procedures must come from the exact vehicle’s repair information, not a generic internet threshold.

Buying BJG from China for Africa

Search Chinese donor stock using BJG and the donor model together. The Chinese joint-venture and imported catalogue records above are separate sourcing leads. A shared code or filter application does not establish assembly interchangeability. Request current photos and an itemized offer for the exact unit.

Ask for a written component list: bare engine or long block, manifolds, fuel components, sensors, wiring, controllers and any transmission supplied. Include turbo or hybrid components only where applicable and explicitly agreed. The words “complete engine” are not a standard packing list.

Before shipment, agree the offered-unit identity, inspection evidence, preservation and packing, shipment terms, destination port and quotation validity. Compare the total agreed scope rather than engine price alone. AsiaPower confirms availability for the specific request; an application guide is not a live-stock guarantee.

Have the receiving workshop in the destination country confirm access to the relevant diagnostic information and needed service parts. Agree installation and commissioning scope, written warranty or inspection-period terms, exclusions, claim evidence, and who bears freight and labour costs. Manufacturer new-car coverage should not be assumed to apply to an independently exported used engine.
